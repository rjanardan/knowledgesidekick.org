Direct answer

What the training is for

Training is for the people who have to run knowledge-first practice after the person who introduced it has gone. It is not a lecture and it is not a textbook. It is a working vocabulary and a set of practice areas, taught in a form the team can actually use.

What the training covers

The training is built around the same ideas as the rest of this site, but taught rather than read. It covers:

  • The core argument — why knowledge is a first-class layer in agentic systems, why it tends to fail first, and what it means to design for it. This is the vocabulary the rest of the training builds on.
  • The four failure modes — stale knowledge, unowned knowledge, no deprecation, and weak grounding. These are the ordinary ways the knowledge layer fails, and the first place to look when an agent goes wrong.
  • Representation and lifecycle — the two standards that usually matter first: how knowledge is shaped so an agent can use it, and how it is kept current and retired when it is no longer true.
  • Context as a delivery problem — how to pack a context window for the right knowledge at the right moment, instead of dumping everything in and letting the model sort it out.
  • Token savings and grounding — the same discipline seen from two sides: cheaper runs and more dependable runs, both the result of taking the knowledge layer seriously.
  • Enterprise and personal context — the different knowledge problems at organizational scale and at human scale, including privacy, control, and compliance under regimes such as the DPDP Act in India and the GDPR in the EU.
  • Adoption practice — how to put knowledge-first practice into a real team, in a real workflow, in a way that survives the people who started it.

The training is not a catalogue. It is a working vocabulary and a set of practice areas, taught in the order that matches the team's situation.

Who the training is for

The training is built for two audiences at once — the same audiences as the site:

  • The person signing off — the leader or manager who is deciding whether a knowledge layer is worth treating as a first-class investment, and who needs the business case without the jargon.
  • The person building — the architect or engineer who is being asked to make an agent reliable, cheap enough to run, and safe enough to use on real data, and who needs the technical vocabulary and the practice.

The training is written so both can read it. The business-case enough for the person signing off, the technical enough for the person building. This is the same discipline as the site itself.

How the training is delivered

The training is delivered in the form that matches the team. The options are:

  • A session for the team — a working session where the ideas are taken apart in front of the people who will run them, with the team's actual knowledge problems as the starting point.
  • A workshop on a specific standard — a focused workshop on representation, lifecycle, grounding, the agent interface, or discoverability, taught against the team's real content and real agents.
  • A reading and discussion pass — a structured read of the site's research pieces, with discussion shaped around the team's situation, for teams that want the ideas first and the practice second.
  • The internship — for individuals who want to work on the ideas in the open, in public, as the entry point to the practice. See the Internship page.

The business-facing side of the training — the engagements, the delivery, the schedule — lives at knowledgesidekick.com. This page is the open, not-for-profit description of what the training is for and what it covers.

Where to start

If you are thinking about training for your team and you do not know which form fits, the usual sequence is:

  • Read Why Knowledge — to get the vocabulary and the argument.
  • Read Adoption — to see what the practice looks like when it is put into place.
  • Look at the Standards — to see which conventions are likely to matter first for your team.
  • Then talk to us about the form that fits — a session, a workshop, a reading pass, or the internship.

The goal of the training is not to teach the site. The goal is to put the practice into the team in a form they can run without the originator in the room. Everything else is detail.
