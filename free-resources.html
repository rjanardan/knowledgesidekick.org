The agent surface and the discoverability layer

The free resources also include the public-facing agent surface — the clean, documented surface that agent engines can rely on to find and use knowledge, as opposed to scraping whatever is easy. This is where things like llms.txt and the discoverability layer live.

The idea is simple: if an agent engine cannot find your knowledge, it cannot act on it. The agent surface and the discoverability layer are the free, open counterpart to the search and SEO work that already exists for human readers. They are part of the standards work, and they are part of the free resources.

The full standard is still a working draft. The public site itself is the first version of the surface — a small, flat, documented site that an agent engine can read and use. The conventions are refined through use, in the open, by the people doing the work.

The business-facing side of the agent surface — the engagements, the delivery, the schedule — lives at knowledgesidekick.com.

How to use these resources

These resources are not a checklist to consume in order. They are a map of the work and a set of starting points. The usual entry points are:

  • Why Knowledge — if you are picking up the argument for the first time.
  • Research — if you want the ideas developed across several angles.
  • Standards — if you want the conventions anyone can adopt.
  • Adoption — if you want the practice of putting the ideas into place.
  • Blogs — if you want the current, opinionated view of what is happening in the field.
  • Community — if you want to take part in the open work with other people.
  • Internship — if you want to work on the ideas in the open, as an individual entry point.
  • Training — if you want the ideas taught in a form your team can run.

The goal is not to make you read everything. The goal is to make the work usable by anyone, for free, without a license gate — and to leave the door open for the business-facing side if and when you want to buy help rather than do it yourself.
